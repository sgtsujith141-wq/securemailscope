## Title

SecureMailScope — Evidence-Backed Cryptographic Investigation for Email
(SIH26159)

## Description

SecureMailScope reads packet captures you already have and reports what the
bytes actually show about how email was transported: which TLS versions were
negotiated, which cipher suites were accepted, which sessions had no forward
secrecy, and what the certificate said — with every finding traced back to the
packets that establish it.

Everything is passive and local. The engine opens no socket. No host in a
capture is ever contacted, no domain is resolved, and no capture content is
transmitted anywhere.

What you will see in this demonstration:

- 0:00 — the problem: encryption is visible, sound cryptography is not
- 0:14 — passive by design
- 0:28 — nine synthetic captures uploaded and analysed at real speed
- 0:52 — the investigation dashboard, and what needs attention first
- 1:25 — a finding, and the exact packets it was evaluated against
- 1:50 — a session's handshake, link by link
- 2:05 — what the tool refuses to guess: TLS 1.3 encrypts the certificate
- 2:20 — cryptographic drift between captures of the same endpoint
- 2:45 — machine learning, with its limits stated on screen
- 3:00 — exporting a forensic report

Every capture in this demonstration is synthetic, generated by the project's
own fixture generator. No real mail traffic, from any person or organisation,
appears at any point. Every hostname uses the reserved `.invalid` TLD
(RFC 2606).

The narration voice is **synthesised** by the operating system's speech
engine. It is not a person. Every line is also burned in as a caption, so the
video is fully usable with the sound off.

Honest limitations, stated in the product and in this video:
- TLS 1.3 encrypts the Certificate message; a passive capture without
  decryption material cannot expose it.
- The supervised risk classifier is `NOT_VALIDATED` for real-world use.
- The anomaly detector in use is a deterministic frequency baseline, not a
  machine-learning model.
- Nothing in this demonstration is an attack. The tool observes
  configurations.
